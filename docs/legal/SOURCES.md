# Legal and Sector Research Sources

> **Research notes only — not legal advice.** Accessed **5 October 2026**. Competitor materials are examples of sector structure, not templates or authority. The drafts paraphrase lessons and do not reproduce competitor clauses.

## Primary UK legal and regulatory sources

### Consumer contracts and online ordering

- [Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134) — distance contracts generally require specified pre-contract information, a durable confirmation and cancellation information. Regulation 28 includes an exception concerning accommodation, transport, vehicle hire, catering and services related to leisure activities for a specific date or period. Whether that exception applies to The Hub's desk or room products requires solicitor analysis.
- [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/part/2?view=plain) — contract confirmation must be supplied on a durable medium within the prescribed time and, for a service, before performance begins.
- [Consumer Rights Act 2015, services](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1/chapter/4/enacted) — consumer services carry statutory standards including reasonable care and skill; statements about the service may become binding; statutory remedies cannot be drafted away.
- [Consumer Rights Act 2015, unfair terms](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/2/2018-07-01) — consumer terms and notices must be fair, transparent and, where relevant, prominent. Liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence cannot be excluded.
- [Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/body/2026-05-07) — online contracting should explain technical steps, permit correction of input errors, acknowledge orders electronically, and make contractual terms storable and reproducible.
- [CMA/GOV.UK: selling goods and services in compliance with consumer law](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-products-and-services-complying-with-consumer-protection-law) — current hub for pricing transparency, optional charges, fair terms, refunds and marketing guidance.
- [CMA/GOV.UK: writing a fair contract](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/writing-a-fair-contract-for-customers) — terms should use clear language and avoid unfair imbalance; legal jargon and hidden significant conditions are poor practice.
- [GOV.UK: online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/distance-selling) — practical summary of information that must be given before and after a distance sale.

### Optional recurring memberships

- [Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Part 4 subscription provisions](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4/2026-01-01?timeline=false) — provides a future/current statutory framework for recurring subscription contracts, including pre-contract information, reminders, cooling-off and ending a subscription. The legislation pages show commencement annotations; obtain current solicitor confirmation before launching membership auto-renewal.
- [DMCCA 2024, meaning of subscription contract](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/body/2026-09-30) — recurring or continuing paid supply with a consumer right to end can fall within the statutory definition when the relevant provisions are in force.
- [DMCCA 2024, Schedule 23 cooling-off information](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/23/paragraph/21/2026-01-01) — illustrates detailed disclosure expected for subscription cooling-off rights; the page is marked prospective and should not be treated as proof of current commencement.

### Privacy and marketing

- [ICO: right-to-be-informed checklists](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/checklists/) — a privacy notice should identify the controller, purposes, lawful bases, recipients, transfers, retention, rights, complaints and relevant automated decisions, using concise and accessible language.
- [ICO: guide to lawful basis](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/) — choose and document the lawful basis before processing; disclose it in privacy information and reassess new purposes.
- [ICO: obtaining, recording and managing consent](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/how-should-we-obtain-record-and-manage-consent/) — consent should be specific, informed, affirmative and documented; keep the wording/version shown at the time. Consent requests should be separate from general terms.
- [ICO: direct marketing checklist](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-self-assessment-medium-businesses/direct-marketing-checklist/) — marketing consent should be unbundled and not pre-ticked; records should show who consented, when, how and to what.
- [ICO: planning direct marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/plan-direct-marketing/) — direct marketing needs an appropriate UK GDPR lawful basis and must also comply with PECR where applicable.

### Payment interface reference

- [Stripe API: Payment Link consent collection](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_links/payment_links/object) — Stripe supports terms-of-service consent collection and separate promotional-consent settings. The Hub must still control and retain the applicable terms version and ensure its complete checkout complies with UK law.

## Official coworking-sector examples

- [Workspace: website and meeting-room terms](https://www.workspace.co.uk/terms-and-conditions) — separates general website terms from meeting-room booking terms and identifies the responsible operating entity, company details and VAT number. Lesson: product-specific contract language and operator identity should be explicit.
- [Workspace: meeting-room privacy notice](https://www.workspace.co.uk/privacy-policy/privacy-notice-meeting-rooms) — uses a service-specific privacy notice. Lesson: explain why booking data is needed and how the meeting-room operation uses it.
- [WeWork On Demand Membership Terms (UK)](https://www.wework.com/en-GB/legal/WeWork-On-Demand-Membership-Terms-and-Conditions) — distinguishes one-time workspace/room bookings, premises rules, included facilities, payment and cancellation windows. Lesson: define the booked entitlement, availability, guest/capacity rules and a usable cancellation mechanism.
- [WeWork On Demand service description](https://www.wework.com/en-GB/solutions/wework-on-demand) — publicly distinguishes day desks, private offices and hourly meeting rooms and explains typical inclusions. Lesson: product scope and room capacity should be clear before purchase.
- [Regus/IWG UK general terms for office, coworking and membership](https://assets.regus.com/terms-and-conditions/en-gb-global-terms-and-conditions.pdf) — separates general contract terms from centre House Rules and addresses access, use, damage, belongings, accommodation allocation and memberships. Lesson: keep day-to-day venue rules readable and aligned with the commercial contract.
- [The Office Group privacy policy](https://www.theofficegroup.com/privacy-policy) — identifies its controller and describes online/offline processing under UK data-protection law. Lesson: the notice must reflect the whole real customer relationship, not only form submissions.
- [The Office Group contact form](https://www.theofficegroup.com/contact) — presents terms/privacy messaging separately from an optional marketing choice. Lesson: do not bundle operational contact or contract acceptance with promotional consent.

## Drafting conclusions adopted in this repository

1. The one-time booking terms, optional membership provisions and House Rules are distinct layers and should not be collapsed into a single unreadable page.
2. Material information—product, date/time, capacity, total, VAT, cancellation deadline and recurring nature—must appear prominently in the journey, not only behind a legal link.
3. Terms/House Rules acceptance must be an affirmative unticked action; optional marketing must be separate and optional.
4. Store the exact accepted versions and send a durable confirmation containing the contract information and cancellation route.
5. Preserve statutory rights and use fair, evidenced remedies rather than blanket exclusions or automatic penalties.
6. Do not publish a categorical cooling-off statement for The Hub's dated bookings until a solicitor classifies the service under the Consumer Contracts Regulations.
7. Do not launch auto-renewing memberships until current subscription-law commencement and operational requirements have been confirmed.
8. The privacy notice must be reconciled against an actual production data map, supplier list, cookie inventory, transfer assessment and retention schedule.
